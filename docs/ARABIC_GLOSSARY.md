# TripEase Arabic glossary (MSA)

**Status: pending native review (Rana).** Not natively reviewed. Decisions below are the working choices behind the Arabic
strings (`lib/l10n/app_ar.arb` in the app, `i18n/ar.json` on the website). Rana's confirmed point is marked **(confirmed)**; everything else is a proposal.

Style rules used throughout: Modern Standard Arabic; direct second person (أنت) in a calm, plain register; no calques ("هبط" for "arrive"), no
chains of "تم + مصدر", "قام بـ" or "الخاص بك" where a verb or possessive does the job; Western digits (0-9); brands stay in Latin letters
(TripEase, Petros Codex); acronyms and official form names stay in Latin letters (ETA, eTA, ESTA, TDAC, eSIM); mirrored arrows (↖ instead of ↗) in right-to-left text.

| English | Arabic | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Land & Sea (the mode and page) | البر والبحر **(confirmed)** | Rana's choice. A fixed product name, written «البر والبحر» in running text on the site. |
| Air (the mode) | الطيران | Names the activity (air travel), parallel to البر والبحر. Alternative "الجو" is less common for travel. |
| Trip | رحلة | Standard word for a journey. "سفرة" is colloquial. |
| Passport | جواز سفر | Standard; always "جواز السفر" with the article, never "باسبور". |
| Visa | تأشيرة | The standard official term in every Arab state. |
| Visa-free | بدون تأشيرة / دون تأشيرة | Plain and common on official pages. "معفى من التأشيرة" is used only for exempt categories of people (معفيّون). |
| e-Visa / eVisa | تأشيرة إلكترونية | Official form: visa + "إلكترونية". Form names such as "e-Arrival Card" stay Latin. |
| Visa on arrival | تأشيرة عند الوصول | Matches the English meaning exactly; the usual wording at Arab and Asian borders. |
| Entry requirements / entry rules | شروط الدخول | Common on official travel advice; "متطلبات" is a direct calque. Legal and official-source text keeps its exact meaning. |

| Cross (a border) | عبر / اعبر | The natural verb; "اجتاز" is formal and heavier. |
| Ferry | عبّارة | The standard Arabic word for a passenger ferry. |

## Notes for the reviewer

- The date, time and number formats use Western digits throughout.
- Names of countries, cities and agencies follow the common Arabic form; the official acronym follows in brackets where it helps (ICA, IRCC).
- Where the English wording is a fixed legal or official-source line (visa, entry, disclaimers), the Arabic keeps its meaning exactly and only the grammar was smoothed.
